Where we're at

We sat down with Lee to understand how Generation is operating today and what the team is making of the AI moment. The conversation was honest: there's genuine curiosity across the team, but also real questions — about what AI actually does well, what the risks are, and what it means for the people doing the work.

What came through clearly is that Generation doesn't need a general AI overview. The team is switched on. What they need is a session that meets them where they are — skips the intro slides, gets into the tools, and works through real problems with AI rather than talking about them in theory.

That's what this proposal is. A full day. Hands-on. Built around Generation's actual work.

A working session, not a presentation.

Most AI training is passive. You sit, you watch demos, you leave with a PDF. A week later, nothing has changed.

This is different. Everyone in the room has their laptop open from the first hour. The exercises are built from real tasks in your business — not invented scenarios. By the end of the day, the team has used AI to do actual work, not just watched someone else do it.

The goal isn't to make the team feel informed about AI. It's to make them better at their jobs because of it — before they leave the room.

Jonathan runs the session directly. No associate, no deck hand. He's in the room for the full day, and available to the team afterward.

The day.

Five parts. Each one builds on the last. The pace adjusts to the room.

Part 1

What AI actually is — and the one formula you need

No jargon, no hype. A clear model for how large language models work — what they can and can't do, and why the gap between what people expect and what AI delivers is usually a prompting problem, not a capability problem.

Then the Prompt Formula. A single, repeatable structure that works across every AI tool the team will ever use:

The team leaves Part 1 with something they can use in the next 10 minutes. Everything that follows builds on it.

Part 2

Where AI fits Generation's work

This part is specific to Generation. We map the actual work the team does — client work, research, communications, reporting, internal operations — and identify where AI creates genuine leverage versus where it doesn't.

Not hypothetical use cases. Real ones. Small group exercises surface ideas that no consultant would have come up with, because they come from the people who do the work every day.

By the end of Part 2, everyone in the room has at least two AI applications they're going to try the following week.

Part 3

Tools in your hands

Hands-on time with the tools that matter. ChatGPT, Claude, and where relevant, Microsoft Copilot. Everyone works through tasks from their own role — not invented scenarios — and compares results side by side.

The point isn't to pick a winner. It's to understand what each tool does well, where they break down, and how to stop fighting the tool and start using it properly.

This is where the Prompt Formula becomes instinct.

Part 4

Build something real

The afternoon shift. Teams take a real problem from the business and build something with AI — a draft, a framework, a workflow, a tool. Something you can actually use, not a slide about something you might use one day.

Jonathan moves between groups. The goal is outputs, not process — everyone leaves with something in their hands that didn't exist when the day started.

Part 5

Risk, strategy, and what's actually coming

The things that get glossed over at every other AI event. What you should never put in a prompt. Data privacy in practice, not in theory. When local LLMs matter and how to think about deploying them. What Fable changes and why Anthropic's latest development is different from the usual model release noise.

And then the strategic question that most organisations are still avoiding: what does an AI strategy actually look like for a business like Generation — beyond "use more AI"?

This is the part that separates a workshop from a working session. It's where the conversation usually gets most interesting.

After the day.

The workshop is where things click. The week after is where they stick.

In the days following the session, Jonathan schedules individual follow-up conversations with key team members. These are 45-minute sessions — no agenda, no slides — focused on whatever came up in the workshop and how it connects to each person's specific role and priorities.

They're not a debrief. They're a working session. Bring a problem you haven't solved yet.
